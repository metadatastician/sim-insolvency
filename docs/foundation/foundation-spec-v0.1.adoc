= Sim Insolvency Foundation Specification v0.1
:toc: left
:reproducible:

== Purpose

Sim Insolvency is an evidence-driven, time-aware practice simulator for
developing and assessing professional reasoning in fictional England and Wales
corporate-insolvency cases. It teaches how to establish what is known, identify
what is not known, compare procedures, manage concurrent work, satisfy
obligations and record a defensible decision.

It is not legal advice, a law firm, an insolvency practice, live case software,
supervised experience, statutory authorisation, a regulated qualification, a
JIEB result, or accredited CPD without separate external approval.

== Product invariants

. Hidden authored reality is immutable during a run and is never delivered to
  an untrusted learner client.
. Learner-visible evidence is monotonic: disclosure can be superseded or
  challenged, but past disclosure is not erased.
. Learner belief is distinct from fact, evidence and normative state.
. Commands are attempts; only validated events change authoritative state.
. The append-only ledger is portable and deterministic.
. Normative scoring is tied to an identified, compatible, reviewed rule pack.
. Competency and case consequence are reported separately.
. More than one route may be defensible on information reasonably available.
. Network collaboration is optional and cannot bypass the command gateway.
. Certificates report completion and assessed performance only.

== Phase A capability

A learner opens `morrow-engineering-001`, reviews initial disclosures, requests
information, records claims/hypotheses/uncertainties/risks, allocates bounded
time and resources, compares informal restructuring, CVA, Administration, CVL,
compulsory-liquidation risk and continued trading/cessation, then selects and
justifies a route.

CVL and Administration have bounded end-to-end paths. CVA and informal
restructuring permit real viability analysis and referral outcomes. Delay can
make compulsory liquidation a terminal or referral consequence. Workstreams
continue concurrently and deterministic scheduled events respond to elapsed
logical time, preserved evidence and resource choices.

At final submission the learner sees unresolved work but no correctness hints.
The post-submission debrief reveals authored reality, chronology, authorities,
obligation handling, alternative defensible routes, competency observations and
case consequences. A digest-bound Certificate of Completion and Assessed
Performance may then be exported.

== Learning model

The learning unit is not a selected answer but a traceable professional act:

----
available evidence -> stated belief and uncertainty -> option comparison
-> authorised action -> event and consequence -> later correction or defence
----

Assessment observes information gathering, use of evidence, procedural
reasoning, proportionality, prioritisation, timeliness, communication and
record quality. Outcomes matter as consequences, not as a substitute for
reasoning evidence.

== Authority policy

The initial jurisdiction is England and Wales and the initial subject is
corporate insolvency. Each scenario declares an authority cut-off and a rule
pack identity. Phase A rules are a small provisional training subset. An
`UNREVIEWED` rule may guide a debrief but cannot support authoritative scoring
or a professional-certification claim. Missing, revoked, incompatible or
mandatory-unreviewed packs invalidate authoritative scoring.

== Privacy and safety

All case people, companies and documents are fictional. There is no telemetry
by default, external AI dependency or need for live insolvency data. Group
networking is explicit opt-in. Exports carry a simulation watermark. Learner
identity may be pseudonymous. Hidden reality and signing secrets remain in the
trusted kernel/facilitator boundary.

== Architecture

AffineScript is architecturally principal. The executable Phase A kernel may
temporarily use Zig behind AffineScript-facing contracts because the inspected
compiler is experimental and unavailable in the current environment. Idris2
defines ABI invariants; typed-wasm is the intended carrier contract. Gossamer
projects state, Burble transports untrusted group envelopes, and VeriSimDB
persists/indexes portable ledger data behind ports.

== Acceptance

This specification is satisfied only by executable evidence: deterministic
replay, branch/golden-run tests, ABI drift checks, scenario validation,
certificate tamper detection and honest `just doctor` output. A missing
toolchain or integration must fail loudly or be reported as optional; it must
never be represented as a completed integration.
