Assessment draws observations from evidence requested or overlooked, claims,
uncertainty, reasons, timing, action order, resource use, communications,
obligation handling and later correction. It supports competent standard and
alternative routes, minimally acceptable routes, common errors and critical
errors. It does not compare a learner only with one hidden ideal path.

Two outputs remain distinct:

* the competency assessment evaluates reasoning and action against information
  reasonably available at the time;
* the case-consequence report describes effects on assets, business, costs,
  evidence, creditors, employees, investigations and elapsed time.

Before submission the learner sees unresolved claims, acknowledged uncertainty,
missed deadlines and unfinished workstreams, but not correctness. After final
submission the debrief may reveal reality, provenance, authority links,
alternatives and missed opportunities. The rubric requires professional and
empirical validation before high-stakes use.
