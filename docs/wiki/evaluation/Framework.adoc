Evaluation asks whether participants can use the simulator, whether recorded
observations support the intended competency interpretations, whether
alternative defensible routes are treated fairly, and whether facilitation,
authority links and debrief are useful. It must also look for harm: misleading
confidence, hidden-truth leakage, inaccessible interaction, privacy loss and
overclaiming.

A pilot protocol records cohort and recruitment, consent and data minimisation,
baseline experience, environment, interventions, completion and dropout,
technical incidents, rubric disagreement, participant feedback and assessor
agreement. Findings must distinguish product defects, scenario defects,
authority disputes and limits of the evaluation design. A positive pilot is
evidence for a decision, not automatic proof of validity or readiness.
